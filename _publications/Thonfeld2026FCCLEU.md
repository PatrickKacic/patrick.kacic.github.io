Remote-sensing-based forest disturbance monitoring in Europe currently relies largely on annual products derived from the long-term Landsat record. We present the first continental-scale forest canopy cover loss (FCCL) product at monthly resolution and 10 m pixel size, based on optical Sentinel2 and Landsat data, covering 2017-2024. Validation shows high overall accuracy (area-weighted accuracy: undisturbed UA 0.98; disturbed UA 0.78; overall F1 0.85). Regional accuracy varies systematically: omission errors dominate in Central Europe, commission errors in North and South-East Europe. Detection latency follows a clear south–north gradient, from same-month detection in the south to mean lags of ~2-month in the north, driven by limited cloud-free optical acquisitions and winter conditions at higher latitudes. Most FCCL disturbances are recorded in winter and early spring, while some Mediterranean countries show pronounced summer losses driven by the fire season. Absolute loss area is largest in North Europe, whereas the relative share of forest affected is highest in South-West Europe. Compared to existing annual optical products and a radar-based near-real-time alerting system, FCCL performs similarly overall but with a more spatially balanced disturbed-class error profile: it offers higher temporal resolution and finer spatial detail than established optical systems, while compensating for the radar-based system's weaknesses in the fire-dominated systems of southern Europe. FCCL thus complements existing pan-European disturbance products and provides a foundation for future multi-sensor fusion towards continuous, year-round forest monitoring.
